Why this is different

Other tools serve one nonprofit. GMS serves a firm.

Their NFP plans help one nonprofit run its books. None of them give an accounting firm a multi-client, multi-grant, multi-billing-model workspace. Three quick scenarios.

01
Serving a book of 15 nonprofit clients across the firm
QuickBooks / Sage NFP

One subscription per nonprofit. Staff log into 15 separate client accounts, export each GL by hand, rebuild allocations in Excel — no cross-client view of the firm's NFP book.

GMS

One firm workspace. All 15 clients onboarded once; every grant imported via a standard GL upload, allocated through the engine, visible side-by-side from day one.

02
A manager wants a Monday-morning view across all 6 of their NFP clients
QuickBooks / Sage NFP

Six separate logins, six separate exports, and a spreadsheet stitched together by hand. By the time the view is ready, the week has moved on.

GMS

One manager portal showing every assigned client side-by-side — budget vs. actual, pending reviews, overdue items — in a single screen, no exports.

03
Onboarding a new staff member onto an NFP engagement
Karmasuite / NFP tools

Staff hunts down the prior senior's spreadsheets, asks where the documents live, and relearns each client's quirks. Ramp-up is days, sometimes weeks.

GMS

Same workbook structure for every engagement. Prior overrides, document vault, and assignment history all visible on day one — ramp-up is hours.

04
A non-specialist staff member runs the engagement
QuickBooks / Sage / Karmasuite

Each tool assumes the user already understands grant accounting, federal compliance, or the software's quirks. Training and senior oversight become the bottleneck.

GMS

Built for staff, not specialists. Plain-language screens, guided workflows, and engine-driven allocations — no grant-accounting background or software training required.

Those tools sell to the nonprofit. GMS is the platform a firm uses to serve many nonprofits at once.
